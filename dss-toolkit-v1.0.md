# <a id="_Toc455748374"></a>INTRODUCTION

This Digital Security Toolkit is designed to help everyday Internet users in Sri Lanka protect 

themselves from cyber threats and respond effectively when incidents occur\. 

Whether you're a student, business owner, employee, or someone who uses the 

Internet for personal, professional and academic purposes, the toolkit will assist you:

- Respond quickly and effectively to cyberattacks
- Protect your personal and professional data
- Prevent common cyber threats
- Know where to report incidents
- Build good digital security habits

You don't need to be a technical expert to follow these guidelines of the tool\. We have

written everything in simple, practical terms that anyone can understand and 

implement\.

# <a id="_Toc1204546606"></a>1\. WHAT TO DO WHEN A CYBERATTACK HAPPENS

## <a id="_Toc1235208701"></a>1\.1 RECOGNIZING A CYBERATTACK

Common Signs You've Been Attacked:

- Your computer is running very slowly or freezing frequently
- You see pop\-up messages demanding payment or threatening you
- Your files have strange extensions, or you cannot open them
- Your antivirus software is disabled or won't start
- You receive notifications of login attempts you didn't make
- Friends or contacts say they received strange messages from you
- Money is missing from your bank account or unauthorized transactions appear
- Your passwords no longer work
- Your device is sending or receiving data when you're not using it
- You see programs or applications you didn't install
- Your web browser has new toolbars or your homepage has changed
- You're redirected to different websites than you intended to visit

## <a id="_Toc1321549747"></a>1\.2 IMMEDIATE ACTIONS \(First 30 Minutes\)

1. __Step 1: Stay Calm and Don't Panic__
	- Take a deep breath \- panicking can lead to mistakes
	- Most cyber incidents can be resolved if handled properly
	- Don't make hasty decisions like paying ransoms immediately
2. __Step 2: Disconnect from the Internet__
	- Unplug your ethernet cable or turn off Wi\-Fi
	- Turn off mobile data on your phone if it's affected
	- This stops the attack from spreading and prevents data theft
	- Do NOT turn off your computer completely yet \(you may lose evidence\)
3. __Step 3: Take Photos and Notes__
	- Use your phone to photograph any suspicious messages or screens
	- Write down:
		- What time you first noticed the problem
		- What you were doing when it happened
		- Any error messages or unusual behavior
		- Any suspicious emails, messages, or links you may have clicked
4. __Step 4: Do NOT Pay Ransom__
	- If you see a ransom message demanding payment, __DO NOT PAY__
	- Paying doesn't guarantee you'll get your data back
	- It encourages criminals to continue attacking others
	- Report the incident to authorities instead
5. __Step 5: Alert Others__
	- If this is a work device, immediately inform your IT department or supervisor
	- If it's a personal device, inform family members who share the same network
	- Warn your contacts if your email or social media may have been compromised

## <a id="_Toc2087615129"></a>1\.3 IMMEDIATE RESPONSE FOR SPECIFIC ATTACKS

- If You Clicked a Suspicious Link or Downloaded Something:
	- Disconnect from the internet immediately
	- Do not enter any passwords or personal information
	- Run a full antivirus scan \(if your antivirus still works\)
	- Change your passwords from a different, clean device
- If You Received a Ransomware Message:
	- Take a photo of the ransom message
	- Do NOT pay the ransom
	- Disconnect from internet and network
	- Do not delete anything
	- Contact SLCERT \(see Section 5\) and file a police report
- If Your Email or Social Media Was Hacked:
	- Try to change your password immediately from a different device
	- Enable two\-factor authentication if you can access the account
	- Check account settings for:
		- Unauthorized email forwarding rules
		- Unknown connected apps or devices
		- Changed recovery email or phone numbers
	- Notify your contacts that your account was compromised
- If Money Was Stolen from Your Bank Account:
	- Immediately call your bank's hotline to freeze your account
	- File a police report within 24 hours
	- Change your online banking passwords from a secure device
	- Review recent transactions and identify unauthorized ones
	- Request a new debit/credit card
- If Your Phone Was Compromised:
	- Turn on airplane mode
	- Change passwords for important accounts from a computer
	- Contact your mobile service provider about potential SIM swap
	- Consider factory resetting your phone after backing up important data
	- Install security updates before restoring data

## <a id="_Toc1153249630"></a>1\.4 ACTIONS WITHIN 24 HOURS

1. __Step 6: Assess What Was Affected__
	- Make a list of what might have been compromised:
		- Which devices were affected
		- What accounts you were logged into
		- What sensitive information was on the device
		- Whether you had financial information stored
		- If you store work or business data on the device
2. __Step 7: Change Your Passwords__
	- Change passwords for all important accounts from a clean device
	- Start with these priorities:
		1. Email accounts
		2. Banking and financial accounts
		3. Social media accounts
		4. Work\-related accounts
		5. Shopping accounts \(Amazon, Daraz, etc\.\)
	- Use strong, unique passwords for each account
	- Don't reuse old passwords
3. __Step 8: Enable Two\-Factor Authentication__
	- Turn on two\-factor authentication \(2FA\) for all accounts that offer it
	- This adds an extra layer of security
	- Even if hackers have your password, they can't access your account without the second factor
4. __Step 9: Check Your Accounts__
	- Review your bank statements for unauthorized transactions
	- Check your email for:
		- Password reset emails you didn't request
		- Notifications of new logins
		- Suspicious forwarding rules
	- Look at your social media for:
		- Posts you didn't make
		-  Messages sent from your account
		- New followers or connections
5. __Step 10: Scan for Malware__
	- Use reputable antivirus software to scan your device
	- Free options include: Windows Defender \(built into Windows\), BitDefender\(Free Version\)
	- Paid options include: Kaspersky, Bitdefender, Norton
	- Run a full system scan, not just a quick scan
6. __Step 11: Back Up Important Data \(If Not Infected\)__
	- If your device is clean, back up important files immediately
	- Use an external hard drive or USB drive
	- Store backups offline \(disconnected from your computer\)
	- This protects you if another attack happens

## <a id="_Toc1750568624"></a>1\.5 ACTIONS WITHIN ONE WEEK

1. __Step 12: Consider Professional Help__

- If you cannot remove the malware yourself, seek help from:
	- Computer repair shops with cybersecurity expertise
	- IT professionals or consultants
	- Your workplace IT department
- If the attack involved significant data loss or financial theft, consider hiring a cybersecurity expert

1. __Step 13: Restore or Rebuild Your System__

- If malware cannot be completely removed, consider:
	- Factory resetting your device \(this erases everything\)
	- Reinstalling your operating system
	- Restoring from a clean backup made before the infection
- Before restoring:
	- Back up any important files \(but scan them for malware first\)
	- Make a list of programs you need to reinstall
	- Save any important bookmarks or settings

1. __Step 14: Update Everything__

- Install all available updates for:
	- Your operating system \(Windows, macOS, Android, iOS\)
	- All applications and programs
	- Your web browser
	- Your antivirus software
- Updates often fix security vulnerabilities that hackers exploit

1. __Step 15: Monitor Your Accounts__

- For the next 30\-90 days, regularly check:
	- Bank accounts and credit card statements
	- Email account activity
	- Social media accounts
	- Credit reports \(if available in Sri Lanka\)
- Set up alerts for:
	- Banking transactions
	- Password changes
	- New device logins

1. __Step 16: Report the Incident__

- File a report with SLCERT \(see Section 5 for detailed information on it\)
- If financial loss occurred, file a police report
- If your work data was affected, report to your employer
- Keep copies of all reports and reference numbers

## <a id="_Toc350226382"></a>1\.6 LEARNING FROM THE INCIDENT

1. __Step 17: Understand What Happened__

- Try to identify how you were attacked:
	- Did you click on a suspicious link?
	- Did you download something unsafe?
	- Did you use a public Wi\-Fi network?
	- Was your password too weak or reused?
- Understanding the cause helps prevent future attacks

1. __Step 18: Improve Your Security__

- Based on what happened, take steps to prevent it from happening again
- Read Section 3 of this toolkit for detailed prevention strategies
- Share your experience with family and friends to help them stay safe

# <a id="_Toc1566990308"></a>2\. HOW TO PROTECT YOUR DATA

## <a id="_Toc889929175"></a>2\.1 UNDERSTANDING YOUR DATA

- What Kind of Data Do You Have?
	- Personal Information:
		- Your name, address, phone number, email
		- National Identity Card \(NIC\) number
		- Driver's license number
		- Passport information
		- Date of birth
	- Financial Information:
		- Bank account numbers
		- Credit/debit card numbers and CVV codes
		- Online banking passwords
		- Mobile money account details \(iPay, eZ Cash, mCash\)
		- Investment or loan information
	- Sensitive Personal Data:
		- Medical records and health information
		- Private photos and videos
		- Personal conversations and messages
		- Location history
		- Biometric data \(fingerprints, face ID\)
	- Professional/Work Data:
		- Work emails and documents
		- Business plans and strategies
		- Client or customer information
		- Trade secrets or proprietary information
		- Login credentials for work systems
- Why Protecting Your Data Matters:
	- Identity theft: Criminals can pretend to be you and commit fraud
	- Financial loss: Direct theft from your accounts or fraudulent loans in your name
	- Privacy violation: Personal information exposed to the public
	- Reputation damage: Private information used to embarrass or blackmail you
	- Security risks: Work data breaches can affect your employer and colleagues

## <a id="_Toc449564880"></a>2\.2 BASIC DATA PROTECTION PRINCIPLES

__Principle 1: Know What You Have__

- Make a list of where you store important information
- Include: computers, phones, tablets, cloud storage, external drives, paper documents
- Know what data is in each location

__Principle 2: Minimize Data Storage__

- Don't keep data you no longer need
- Delete old files, emails, and messages regularly
- Avoid storing sensitive information unless necessary
- The less data you have, the less you can lose

__Principle 3: Control Who Has Access__

- Only share data with people who truly need it
- Use privacy settings on social media
- Don't share passwords with others
- Lock your devices with passwords or PINs

__Principle 4: Protect Data in Multiple Ways__

- Use passwords and encryption
- Keep backups in safe locations
- Use security software
- Stay aware of threats and scams

__Principle 5: Have a Recovery Plan__

- Know how you'll recover if data is lost or stolen
- Keep backup copies
- Know how to reset accounts and passwords
- Document important account information \(securely\)

## <a id="_Toc1361150509"></a>2\.3 PASSWORD PROTECTION

- Creating Strong Passwords:
	- What Makes a Good Password?
		- At least 12\-16 characters long \(longer is better\)
		- Mix of uppercase and lowercase letters
		- Includes numbers and special characters \(\!@\#$%^&\*\)
		- NOT based on personal information \(name, birthday, pet names\)
		- NOT common words or phrases
		- Unique for each account \(never reuse passwords\)
	- Examples of Weak Passwords to AVOID:
		- password123
		- your name or family names
		- your birthday or anniversary
		- 123456 or qwerty
		- srilanka or colombo
		- Your NIC number
	- Creating Memorable Strong Passwords:
		- Method 1 \- Passphrase: String together random words
			- Example: "Purple\-Elephant\-Dances\-Moonlight\-47"
		- Method 2 \- Sentence Method: Take first letters of a sentence
			- Example: "I graduated from University of Colombo in 2015\!"
			- Becomes: "IgfUoCi2015\!"
		- Method 3 \- Substitution Method: Replace letters with numbers/symbols
			- Example: "SriLanka" becomes "Sr\!L@nk@2024”
	- Using a Password Manager:
		- Password managers safely store all your passwords
		- You only need to remember one master password
		- They can generate strong random passwords for you
		- Free options: Bitwarden, KeePassXC
		- Paid options: 1Password, LastPass, Dashlane
	- Benefits:
		- Use unique passwords for every account without memorizing them
		- Automatically fill in passwords on websites
		- Sync passwords across your devices
		- More secure than writing passwords on paper or in notes
	- How to Use a Password Manager:
		- Download and install a reputable password manager
		- Create one very strong master password
		- Add your existing accounts and passwords
		- Generate new strong passwords for each account
		- Access your passwords whenever you need them
	- Two\-Factor Authentication \(2FA\):
		- What Is It?
			- A second layer of security beyond your password
			- Requires something you know \(password\) \+ something you have \(phone or security key\)
			- Even if someone steals your password, they can't access your account
	- Types of 2FA:
		- SMS codes: You receive a text message with a code
		- Authenticator apps: Apps generate time\-based codes \(Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy\)
		- Email codes: Codes sent to your email
		- Biometrics: Fingerprint or face recognition
		- Security keys: Physical USB devices \(most secure\)
	- Which Accounts Need 2FA? \(Priority Order\)
		- Email accounts \(Gmail, Yahoo, etc\.\)
		- Banking and mobile money apps
		- Social media \(Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp\)
		- Work accounts
		- Shopping accounts
		- Any account with personal or financial information
	- How to Set Up 2FA:
		- Go to your account security settings
		- Look for "Two\-Factor Authentication," "2\-Step Verification," or "Security"
		- Choose your preferred method \(authenticator app recommended over SMS\)
		- Follow the setup instructions
		- Save backup codes in a safe place
		- Test it by logging out and back in
	- Password Don'ts:
		- Never share your passwords with anyone \(even family, unless absolutely necessary\)
		- Never write passwords on sticky notes on your desk
		- Never save passwords in your browser on shared computers
		- Never use the same password for multiple important accounts
		- Never email or message your passwords to others
		- Never include your password in the answer to security questions

## <a id="_Toc1284896113"></a>2\.4 ENCRYPTION AND SECURE STORAGE

- What Is Encryption?
	- Encryption scrambles your data so only authorized people can read it
	- It's like putting your data in a locked safe
	- Even if someone steals the encrypted data, they can't use it without the key
- Encrypting Your Devices:
- Windows Computers:
	- Use BitLocker \(available on Windows Pro/Enterprise\)
	- Steps:
		-   Search for "BitLocker" in Windows
		-   Click "Turn on BitLocker"
		-   Choose how to unlock your drive \(password or USB key\)
		-   Save your recovery key in a safe place
		-   Follow the prompts to encrypt your drive
- Mac Computers:
	- Use FileVault \(built into macOS\)
	- Steps:
		-  Go to System Preferences > Security & Privacy
		-  Click the FileVault tab
		-  Click "Turn On FileVault"
		-  Save your recovery key safely
		-  Restart your computer to begin encryption
- Android Phones:
	- Most modern Android phones are encrypted by default
	- To check:
		- Go to Settings > Security > Encryption
		- If not encrypted, follow prompts to encrypt
		- Choose a strong PIN or password
- iPhones/iPads:
	- Automatically encrypted when you set a passcode
	- Make sure you have a strong passcode:
		- Settings > Face ID & Passcode \(or Touch ID & Passcode\)
		- Change Passcode
		- Use a 6\-digit or custom alphanumeric code
- Encrypting USB Drives and External Hard Drives:
	- Use VeraCrypt \(free, open\-source\)
	- Steps:
		- Download VeraCrypt from veracrypt\.fr
		- Install and open VeraCrypt
		- Click "Create Volume"
		- Choose "Encrypt a non\-system partition/drive"
		- Select your USB drive
		- Choose a strong password
		- Follow the wizard to complete encryption
- __Important: Save Recovery Keys\!__
	- When you encrypt devices, you'll receive recovery keys
	- Store these safely \- if you lose them AND forget your password, your data is lost forever
	- Write them down and keep in a secure location \(safe, locked drawer\)
	- Consider giving a copy to a trusted family member
- Protecting Files on Your Computer:
	- For Individual Files:
		- Use 7\-Zip \(free\) to create password\-protected archives
		- Steps:
			- Download and install 7\-Zip
			- Right\-click the file or folder
			- Choose 7\-Zip > Add to archive
			- Set a strong password in encryption section
			- Choose AES\-256 encryption
			- Click OK to create encrypted archive
	- Cloud Storage Encryption:
		- Most cloud services \(Google Drive, Dropbox\) encrypt your data
		- However, they hold the encryption keys
		- For extra security, encrypt files before uploading
		- Use Cryptomator \(free\) for encrypting cloud storage
	- Secure Deletion of Data:
		- Simply deleting files doesn't completely remove them
		- They can often be recovered with special software
		- To permanently delete sensitive files:
			- On Windows:
				- Use Eraser \(free tool\)
				- Download from eraser\.heidi\.ie
				- Right\-click files and choose "Eraser > Erase”
			- On Mac:
				- Use "Secure Empty Trash" feature
				- Or use permanent erase command in Terminal
			- On Mobile:
				- Factory reset before selling or disposing of devices
				- Encrypt the device first, then factory reset for maximum security

## <a id="_Toc393772516"></a>2\.5 BACKING UP YOUR DATA

- Why Backups Are Critical:
	- Protects against ransomware \(you don't need to pay if you have backups\)
	- Recovers data after device theft or loss
	- Restores files after accidental deletion
	- Protects against hardware failure
- The 3\-2\-1 Backup Rule:
	- Keep at least 3 copies of your data
	- Store copies on 2 different types of media \(computer, external drive, cloud\)
	- Keep 1 copy offsite or offline \(not connected to your computer\)
- What to Back Up:
	- Essential Data \(Back up weekly\):
		- Important documents \(IDs, certificates, contracts\)
		- Financial records and tax documents
		- Photos and videos
		- Work files and projects
		- Contact lists
		- Passwords \(from password manager\)
- How to Back Up:
	- Method 1: External Hard Drive or USB Drive
		- Advantages:
			- You own and control the data
			- Fast to restore
			- No internet required
			- One\-time cost
		- Disadvantages:
			- Can be lost, stolen, or damaged
			- Requires manual backup
			- Not accessible remotely
		- Steps:
			- Buy an external hard drive \(at least 1TB recommended\)
			- Connect to your computer
			- Copy important folders to the drive
			- OR use backup software:
				- Windows: File History or Backup and Restore
				- Mac: Time Machine
			- Keep the drive in a safe place when not backing up
			- Disconnect after backing up \(protects from ransomware\)
	- Method 2: Cloud Storage
		- Popular Services:
			- Google Drive \(15GB free\)
			- Dropbox \(2GB free, more with referrals\)
			- OneDrive \(Microsoft, 5GB free\)
			- iCloud \(Apple, 5GB free\)
		- Advantages:
			- Accessible from anywhere
			- Automatic backups
			- Protected from physical damage or theft
			- Often includes version history
		- Disadvantages:
			- Requires internet connection
			- Ongoing subscription costs for large storage
			- Privacy concerns \(company has access to your data\)
			- Dependent on company staying in business
		- Steps:
			- Choose a cloud service
			- Create an account
			- Install desktop app or use web browser
			- Select folders to sync automatically
			- Important: Encrypt sensitive files before uploading
	- Method 3: Combination Approach \(Recommended\)
		- Keep a local backup on external drive \(fast access\)
		- Keep a cloud backup \(offsite protection\)
		- Store a quarterly backup drive at a friend's or family member's house
	- Backup Schedule:
		- Daily: If you work with important data every day
		- Weekly: For most personal users
		- Monthly: At minimum for everyone
		- Before major events: Before travels, major purchases, or important work
	- Mobile Phone Backups:
		- Android:
			- Use Google Backup:
				- Settings > Google > Backup
				- Turn on "Back up to Google Drive"
				- Select what to back up \(apps, photos, etc\.\)
		- iPhone:
			- Use iCloud Backup:
				- Settings > \[Your Name\] > iCloud > iCloud Backup
				- Turn on iCloud Backup
				- Tap "Back Up Now”
	- Also consider:
		- Using Google Photos or similar to back up photos
		- Manually copying important files to computer
		- Exporting contacts regularly
	- Testing Your Backups:
		- Backups are useless if they don't work when you need them
		- Test restoring a few files every few months
		- Verify backups completed successfully
		- Check that all important data is included

## <a id="_Toc1303643270"></a>2\.6 PROTECTING DATA ON MOBILE DEVICES

- Mobile Device Security Settings:
	- Screen Lock:
		- Always use a screen lock
		- Options \(from most to least secure\):
			- Alphanumeric password \(best\)
			- 6\+ digit PIN
			- Pattern \(make it complex\)
			- Fingerprint or Face ID \(use with PIN backup\)
		- Set auto\-lock to 1\-2 minutes
- Biometric Security:
	- Fingerprint and face recognition are convenient and reasonably secure
	- Always set up a backup PIN/password
	- Note: Authorities may be able to compel you to unlock with biometrics in some situations
- Find My Device:
	- Enable this feature to locate, lock, or erase your phone remotely if lost or stolen
		- For Android:
			- Settings > Security > Find My Device
			- Turn it on
			- Sign in to android\.com/find from any browser to locate your device
		- For iPhone:
			- Settings > \[Your Name\] > Find My
			- Turn on "Find My iPhone"
			- Visit icloud\.com/find to locate your device
- App Permissions:
	- Apps often request more permissions than they need
	- Review and limit permissions:
		- Android:
			- Settings > Privacy > Permission Manager
			- Review each permission type
			- Remove unnecessary permissions
		- iPhone:
			- Settings > Privacy
			- Review each permission category
			- Adjust as needed
- Only grant permissions that make sense:
	- Camera app needs camera access ✓
	- Flashlight app does NOT need location access ✗
	- Calculator app does NOT need contacts access ✗
- Mobile Data Protection:
	- Don't store sensitive information in notes or photos
	- Use secure apps for sensitive communication \(Signal, WhatsApp\)
	- Enable app\-specific passwords for banking and payment apps
	- Log out of banking apps when not in use
	- Don't save passwords in mobile browsers
- Before Selling or Disposing:
	- Back up your data
	- Sign out of all accounts
	- Remove SIM card and memory card
	- Factory reset the device
	- For iPhones, also remove from your Apple ID account

## <a id="_Toc992723863"></a>2\.7 SOCIAL MEDIA PRIVACY

- Privacy Settings to Check:
- Facebook:
	- Go to Settings & Privacy > Settings > Privacy
	- Set "Who can see your future posts?" to Friends \(not Public\)
	- Limit past posts visibility
	- Control who can send friend requests
	- Review timeline and tagging settings
	- Turn off location services for Facebook
	- Review apps and websites connected to your account
	- Set up login alerts
- Instagram:
	- Go to Settings > Privacy
	- Consider switching to Private Account
	- Control who can see your story
	- Disable activity status if you don't want others to know when you're online
	- Review accounts you're sharing data with
- WhatsApp:
	- Settings > Account > Privacy
	- Set "Last Seen" to "Nobody" or "My Contacts"
	- Set "Profile Photo" to "My Contacts"
	- Set "About" to "My Contacts"
	- Disable read receipts if desired
	- Enable two\-step verification
	- Turn off automatic media download on cellular data
- General Social Media Safety:

## <a id="_Toc783876185"></a>3\.2 EMAIL SECURITY

- Recognizing Phishing Emails:
	- Red Flags to Watch For:
		- Urgent language: "Act now\!" "Account will be closed\!" "Verify immediately\!"
		- Threatening tone: "Your account has been compromised\!" "Legal action will be taken\!"
		- Too good to be true: "You've won a prize\!" "Unclaimed inheritance\!"
		- Generic greetings: "Dear Customer" instead of your name
		- Suspicious sender: Email doesn't match the company \(paypa1\.com instead of paypal\.com\)
		- Spelling and grammar errors
		- Requests for personal information
		- Unexpected attachments
		- Links that don't match the stated destination
	- How to Check Links Safely:
		- Hover your mouse over links \(don't click\) to see the real URL
		- Look for misspellings in the domain name
		- Check that it uses HTTPS \(secure connection\)
		- When in doubt, go directly to the website instead of clicking the link
	- What to Do with Suspicious Emails:
		- Don't click any links or download attachments
		- Don't reply to the email
		- Mark as spam or phishing
		- Delete the email
		- If it claims to be from a company you use, contact them directly using official contact info
		- Report phishing to your email provider
	- Safe Email Practices:
		- Use separate email accounts for different purposes:
			- One for important accounts \(banking, work\)
			- One for shopping and online services
			- One for newsletters and promotions
		- Don't open attachments unless you're expecting them
		- Verify sender before responding to requests
		- Be cautious of emails asking you to take urgent action
		- Enable spam filtering in your email settings
		- Never send sensitive information \(passwords, IDs\) via email
		- Use "BCC" when sending to multiple people to protect privacy
	- Attachment Safety:
		- Safe file types \(usually\):
			- \.pdf \(but be cautious\)
			- \.jpg, \.png, \.gif \(images\)
			- \.txt \(plain text\)
		- Dangerous file types \(avoid\):
			- \.exe \(programs\)
			- \.zip or \.rar \(compressed files from unknown sources\)
			- \.scr \(screensavers\)
			- \.bat \(batch files\)
			- Office files with macros enabled \(\.docm, \.xlsm\)
	- Before opening any attachment:
		- Verify the sender is legitimate
		- Scan with antivirus software
		- Consider opening in a sandbox or online viewer first

## <a id="_Toc655144077"></a>3\.4 WI\-FI AND NETWORK SECURITY

### <a id="_Toc1503104126"></a>Home Wi\-Fi Security:

1. Securing Your Router:

- Change Default Admin Password:
	- Access router settings \(usually 192\.168\.1\.1 in browser\)
	- Find admin settings
	- Create a strong unique password
	- Never use default passwords like "admin"

2\. Change Default Wi\-Fi Network Name \(SSID\):

- Don't use your name or address
- Choose something generic
- This makes you less of a target

3\. Set Strong Wi\-Fi Password:

- At least 12 to 16 characters
- Use WPA3 encryption \(or WPA2 if WPA3 unavailable\)
- Never use WEP \(very insecure\)

4\. Update Router Firmware:

- Check manufacturer's website for updates
- Or check in router admin panel
- Update at least quarterly

5\. Disable WPS \(Wi\-Fi Protected Setup\):

- This feature is convenient but insecure
- Turn it off in router settings

6\. Enable Router Firewall:

- Usually enabled by default
- Verify in router security settings

7\. Create Guest Network:

- Separate network for visitors
- Keeps guests away from your main devices
- Set different password

8\. Disable Remote Management:

- Unless you specifically need it
- Prevents attackers from accessing router from internet

### <a id="_Toc1272581835"></a>Public Wi\-Fi Safety:

1. Risks of Public Wi\-Fi:
	1. Others can see what you're doing
	2. Fake Wi\-Fi networks \(evil twins\)
	3. Unencrypted connections
	4. Man\-in\-the\-middle attacks
2. Safe Public Wi\-Fi Practices:
	1. Avoid accessing sensitive accounts \(banking, email\)
	2. Use your mobile data instead when possible
	3. Always use a VPN on public Wi\-Fi \(see below\)
	4. Verify the network name with staff
	5. Turn off auto\-connect to networks
	6. Disable file sharing
	7. Turn off Wi\-Fi when not in use
	8. Use HTTPS websites only
	9. Log out when finished
3. Using a VPN \(Virtual Private Network\):
	1. What Is a VPN?
		1. Creates a secure, encrypted tunnel for your internet connection
		2. Hides your activity from others on the network
		3. Makes you appear to be in a different location
	2. When to Use a VPN:
		1. Whenever you're on public Wi\-Fi
		2. When accessing work systems remotely
		3. When you want extra privacy
		4. In countries with internet restrictions
	3. Choosing a VPN:
		1. Reputable paid services:
			1. NordVPN
			2. ExpressVPN
			3. ProtonVPN \(has free tier\)
			4. Surfshark
		2. Avoid:
			1. Free VPNs \(often sell your data or inject ads\)
			2. Unknown or suspicious VPN providers
			3. VPNs from countries with poor privacy laws
		3. How to Use a VPN:
			1. Subscribe to a reputable VPN service
			2. Download their app
			3. Install on your devices
			4. Open the app and click "Connect"
			5. Your traffic is now encrypted
4. Mobile Data Security:
	1. Mobile data \(4G/5G\) is generally safer than public Wi\-Fi
	2. However, still use HTTPS websites
	3. Be aware of your data usage and costs
	4. Turn off mobile data when not needed to save battery
	5. Monitor your mobile bill for unexpected charges

## <a id="_Toc1656002375"></a>3\.7 RECOGNIZING AND AVOIDING SCAMS

Common Scams in Sri Lanka:

1\. Phishing Scams:

What it is:

Fake emails, messages, or websites pretending to be legitimate

Goal: Steal your passwords, personal information, or money

Examples:

 "Your bank account has been locked\. Click here to verify\."

 "You've won a prize\! Provide your details to claim\."

 "Your package is delayed\. Update shipping information\."

 "Suspicious activity on your account\. Verify now\."

How to protect yourself:

Never click links in unexpected emails or messages

Verify with the organization directly using official contact info

Check sender email address carefully

Look for poor grammar and spelling

Don't provide personal information via email

2\. Romance Scams:

What it is:

Scammer builds online relationship then asks for money

Often claims emergency or travel needs

Warning signs:

Moves relationship very quickly

Professes love unusually fast

Never wants to meet in person or video call

Always has excuses

Eventually asks for money

Has emergencies requiring financial help

How to protect yourself:

Be skeptical of online relationships

Never send money to someone you haven't met

Reverse image search their photos

Be cautious if they claim to be overseas

Talk to friends and family about the relationship

3\. Job Scams:

What it is:

Fake job offers requiring upfront payment

 "Work from home" scams

Pyramid schemes disguised as jobs

Warning signs:

Promises of easy money with little work

Requires payment for training, materials, or processing

Asks for bank details before hiring

Vague job description

Poor communication or many errors

Pressure to act quickly

Too good to be true salary

How to protect yourself:

Research the company thoroughly

Never pay for a job opportunity

Be suspicious of unsolicited job offers

Verify job postings on official company websites

Check company reviews online

Meet in person at company office if possible

4\. Investment Scams:

What it is:

Promises of high returns with little risk

Ponzi schemes

Cryptocurrency scams

Fake investment opportunities

Warning signs:

Guaranteed high returns

Pressure to invest quickly

"Limited time" opportunities

Requires recruiting others

Unregistered investment schemes

Complex or vague investment strategy

Difficulty withdrawing money

How to protect yourself:

Be skeptical of guaranteed returns

Research investment opportunities thoroughly

Verify with Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka

Never invest money you can't afford to lose

Get advice from independent financial advisor

If it sounds too good to be true, it probably is

5\. Technical Support Scams:

What it is:

Scammers claim your computer has problems

Offer to "fix" issues for a fee

May actually install malware

Warning signs:

Unsolicited calls about computer problems

Pop\-ups claiming computer is infected

Requests for remote access to your computer

Pressure to pay immediately

Claims to be from Microsoft, Apple, or antivirus company

How to protect yourself:

Legitimate companies don't call unsolicited

Never give remote access to unsolicited callers

Close suspicious pop\-ups without clicking

Hang up on suspicious calls

Contact companies directly using official numbers

6\. Lottery and Prize Scams:

What it is:

Claims you've won a prize or lottery you didn't enter

Requires payment or personal information to claim

Warning signs:

You didn't enter any contest or lottery

Requests payment for taxes or processing fees

Asks for bank account or credit card information

Creates urgency \("claim within 24 hours"\)

Communication is via email or social media, not official channels

How to protect yourself:

You can't win a lottery you didn't enter

Never pay to receive a prize

Don't provide personal or financial information

Research the organization online

Legitimate lotteries don't ask for upfront payment

7\. Social Media Scams:

What it is:

Fake accounts impersonating friends or celebrities

Fake giveaways and contests

Scam advertisements

Warning signs:

Friend requests from people you're already friends with

Messages asking for money or personal information

Giveaways requiring payment or extensive personal info

Accounts with few followers or recent creation date

How to protect yourself:

Verify unexpected messages through another channel

Check account details \(followers, posts, creation date\)

Don't click suspicious links from social media

Report fake accounts

Be skeptical of giveaways requiring too much information

General Scam Protection:

Trust your instincts \- if something feels wrong, it probably is

Never make decisions under pressure

Research before taking action

Verify independently through official channels

Don't be embarrassed to ask for help or advice

Remember: Scammers are professional manipulators

It's okay to say no or hang up

8\. GLOSSARY OF TERMS

Common Cybersecurity Terms Explained:

2FA / Two\-Factor Authentication:

Security method requiring two types of verification to access an account \(like 

password \+ phone code\)\.

Antivirus:

Software that detects and removes malicious software \(viruses, malware\)\.

Backup:

A copy of your data stored separately so you can recover it if lost\.

Botnet:

Network of infected computers controlled by hackers\.

Breach / Data Breach:

Incident where unauthorized person accesses protected data\.

Cookie:

Small file websites store on your device to remember you and track activity\.

DDoS \(Distributed Denial of Service\):

Attack that overwhelms a website with traffic to make it unavailable\.

Encryption:

Converting data into code that only authorized people can read\.

Firewall:

Security system that blocks unauthorized access while allowing legitimate 

communication\.

Hacking:

Unauthorized access to computer systems or data\.

HTTPS:

Secure version of HTTP \- encrypts communication between your browser and 

websites \(look for padlock icon\)\.

Identity Theft:

Crime where someone steals your personal information to impersonate you\.

IP Address:

Unique numerical address that identifies your device on the internet\.

Malware:

Malicious software designed to damage or gain unauthorized access \(includes 

viruses, trojans, spyware, ransomware\)\.

Phishing:

Fraudulent attempt to obtain sensitive information by pretending to be 

trustworthy\.

Ransomware:

Malware that encrypts your files and demands payment to unlock them\.

Social Engineering:

Manipulation technique to trick people into giving up confidential information\.

Spam:

Unwanted, irrelevant, or inappropriate messages sent in bulk\.

Spyware:

Software that secretly monitors your activities and collects information\.

Trojan / Trojan Horse:

Malware disguised as legitimate software\.

Virus:

Malware that spreads by copying itself to other programs or files\.

VPN \(Virtual Private Network\):

Service that creates encrypted connection over the internet for privacy and 

security\.

Vulnerability:

Weakness in software or system that can be exploited by attackers\.

Zero\-Day:

Previously unknown vulnerability that hackers exploit before developers can 

fix it\.
